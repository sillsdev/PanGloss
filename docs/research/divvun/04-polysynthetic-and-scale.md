### How recursive/unbounded derivation is bounded

This is the central finding for the "polysynthesis" question. Greenlandic
verbs derive noun→verb→noun→verb chains indefinitely in principle
(e.g. the comment trail at
`lang-kal/src/fst/morphology/affixes/derivations-inflections.lexc:112213-112214`
shows a 3-deep derivation chain `+GUSUP+Der/vv+SIMA+Der/vv+GALUAR+Der/vv`).
GiellaLT does **not** bound this with a generic depth-counter flag diacritic
(no `@P.Depth1.ON@`/`@P.Depth2.ON@`-style ladder was found anywhere in the
file). Instead, two concrete mechanisms are used, both directly VERIFIED by
grep:

1. **Duplicated, pruned continuation classes.** Where one specific
   derivational morpheme (`TIP`) is known to recurse pathologically, the
   grammar writer creates a *second copy* of the continuation lexicon with
   that one morpheme's entry point removed, e.g.
   `LEXICON XIiXmorf_UdenTIP !!= * @CODE@ som XIiXmorf, men uden TIP for at
   blokere rekursive TIP` ("like XIiXmorf, but without TIP to block recursive
   TIP") at `derivations-inflections.lexc:39110`, and it is wired in at
   `derivations-inflections.lexc:125744-125745`:
   `+Gram/Db: TV_UdenTIP ; !Nyt lex 20211130 til forebyggelse af rekursive TIP`
   ("new lexicon … for prevention of recursive TIP") and
   `+Gram/Refl@U.Refl.On@:@U.Refl.On@ XIiXmorf_UdenTIP ; !Nyt LEX … som
   forebygger rekursive TIP i Refl`. This is a finite, hand-maintained
   whitelist/blacklist of which continuation graph a given derived form is
   allowed to re-enter, not a generic recursion guard.
2. **A previous attempt at flag-diacritic recursion blocking was abandoned.**
   `derivations-inflections.lexc:27590` still carries the comment `!test P- og
   D-flag til undgåelse af rekursiv TIP-tagging PL 20200709. PL20240830
   tilføjet C-flag` ("test of P/D-flag to avoid recursive TIP tagging …
   C-flag added"), and `root.lexc:827-835` shows the flag-diacritic version
   commented out (`!@P.TIP.ON@` / `!@D.TIP@`) in favor of the current
   `@U.tip.ON@`/`@U.tip.OFF@` unification-flag pair — i.e. the team tried a
   generic flag-based recursion guard and moved to per-morpheme lexicon
   duplication instead, at least for this affix family. INFERRED: this
   suggests flag-diacritic depth/cycle guards were judged less
   maintainable than explicit lexicon forking, for this grammar.
3. Sixteen distinct comment lines across the file carry the phrase
   `"forsøg på at tillade passive nominer uden at skabe rekursivitet"`
   ("attempt to allow passive nominals without creating recursivity"), e.g.
   `derivations-inflections.lexc:78549-78550`, `:81204-81205`,
   `:99738-99739` — each pairs a `TV-mod_r` (verb-continuing) entry with a
   `Z1_kaZmorf` (noun-terminating) entry for the same derivational suffix, so
   that a specific suffix can be reached from a verb chain and still
   terminate into a noun, without being wired back into the general
   recursive verb lexicon. Again: hand-crafted, per-suffix, not a generic
   bound.

**Net finding (VERIFIED across dozens of citations above):** in the single
most productive derivational language in this survey, GiellaLT's control of
"unbounded" morphology is *not* a generic cycle-depth cap expressed as a
recipe-style gate. It is **per-morpheme graph surgery**: whenever a
derivational suffix is found (empirically, via corpus/native-speaker
feedback — several comments cite specific real word forms as the
motivating example, e.g. `!oqarseqqartunit oqar+Gram/IV+SIR+Der/vv+…`) to
recurse in an unwanted way, the grammar writer forks the continuation
lexicon for that one suffix and removes the offending re-entry point. This
does generalize to a "Gate"-like primitive (a boolean condition on which
paths may continue), but it is applied lexicon-by-lexicon and
suffix-by-suffix, not as a single global depth counter. There is no evidence
in this file of a uniform maximum-derivation-depth mechanism.

### Flag diacritics as compounding/position gates (documented but currently unused for kal)

`root.lexc:1542-1569` (Multichar_Symbols section) documents a **generic,
shared, cross-language flag-diacritic vocabulary** for controlling
compounding position, all currently commented out (`!`-prefixed) in the
Greenlandic grammar itself but described as boilerplate every GiellaLT
language can opt into:

```
!@P.CmpFrst.FALSE@ | Require that words tagged as such only appear first
!@D.CmpPref.TRUE@  | Block such words from entering ENDLEX
!@P.CmpPref.FALSE@ | Block these words from making further compounds
!@D.CmpLast.TRUE@  | Block such words from entering R
!@D.CmpNone.TRUE@  | Combines with the next tag to prohibit compounding
!@U.CmpNone.FALSE@ | Combines with the prev tag to prohibit compounding
!@P.CmpOnly.TRUE@  | Sets a flag to indicate that the word has passed R
!@D.CmpOnly.FALSE@ | Disallow words coming directly from root.
```
(`lang-kal/src/fst/morphology/root.lexc:1562-1569`, VERIFIED)

These same flags are **actively used** in Finnish — see §3.

### Compounding: the actual mechanism, live and in use

`lang-fin/src/fst/morphology/root.lexc:20-51` (VERIFIED, full file read):

```lexc
LEXICON NOUN_COMPOUND ! all nom and gen etc. nouns from fst
     +Use/Circ+Use/NoHyphens:# R_NOMINAL ;
 +Use/Circ+Use/Hyphen+Use/NG:# R_NOMINAL ;
                   +Use/Circ:# R_NOMINAL ;
                               RHyph ;

LEXICON RHyph
                   +Use/Circ:-  R_NOMINAL ;
                   - # ;

LEXICON R_NOMINAL
@P.CmpFrst.FALSE@@P.CmpPref.FALSE@@D.CmpLast.TRUE@@D.CmpNone.TRUE@@U.CmpNone.FALSE@@P.CmpOnly.TRUE@ NOMINAL ;

LEXICON NOMINAL
!! The compound part sub-set `@LEXNAME@`
!! The nominal forms can be used as non-initial parts of typical compounds
+Cmp#:   NOUN_Cmp           ;
```

This is exactly the flag-diacritic vocabulary Greenlandic documents but
leaves unused (§2) — Finnish is the language that actually wires it up. The
mechanism: entering `R_NOMINAL` sets *all six* compounding flags at once
(`CmpFrst.FALSE`, `CmpPref.FALSE`, `CmpLast.TRUE`, `CmpNone.TRUE`,
`CmpOnly.TRUE` set/cleared together), which is how a single nominal
continuation class can serve as "the non-initial part of a compound" while
disallowing further recursive compounding beyond what the flags permit. A
dedicated `+Cmp#:` boundary tag (visible in the tag inventory, e.g.
`lang-fin/src/fst/morphology/root.lexc` tag documentation, and the
`filters/convert_to_flags-CmpNP-tags.regex` /
`filters/split-CmpNP-tags.regex` filter pair used at build time — see §5) is
the surface marker for a compound boundary, later converted into flag
diacritics and back by dedicated filter transducers rather than left as an
uncontrolled lexc cycle. `compounding.lexc` itself
(`lang-fin/src/fst/morphology/compounding.lexc`, 51 lines, VERIFIED, full
file read) holds only the "circular lexicon" entry point (`NOUN_COMPOUND`)
and the hyphenation-variant handling (`RHyph`) — the actual compounding
*restriction* logic lives in `root.lexc`'s flag diacritics, not in
`compounding.lexc`.

**Conclusion for the compounding question (VERIFIED across both repos):**
GiellaLT's answer to "how do you keep productive compounding from destroying
the analyzer" is: (a) a **shared, documented, six-flag vocabulary**
(`CmpFrst`/`CmpPref`/`CmpLast`/`CmpNone`/`CmpOnly`, all flag-diacritic pairs,
not counters) that any language can opt into; (b) a dedicated `+Cmp#:`
lexc entry point that funnels all compound-medial nominal forms through one
restricted sub-lexicon (`NOUN_Cmp`, `R_NOMINAL`) rather than looping back to
the unrestricted `Root`; (c) dedicated pre/post-processing filter
transducers (`convert_to_flags-CmpNP-tags`, `split-CmpNP-tags`) that convert
between a `+Cmp#` string tag (used during lexc compilation, for readability
and for downstream taggers) and the flag-diacritic encoding (used to actually
constrain the FST). This is a flag/gate mechanism, structurally close to
our `Gate` primitive, but it is a **fixed, shared, six-flag idiom reused
verbatim across languages** rather than something built fresh per grammar.

## 5. Build strategy at scale — the actual Makefile evidence

This is the highest-value section for our own recipe design, because I found
the literal build rules, not a description of them.

### 5.1 Lexc compilation: one shot, not per-sublexicon

Contrary to an assumption that GiellaLT compiles each sub-lexicon separately,
the standard giella-core template **concatenates every lexc source file into
one file and compiles it with a single `hfst-lexc` invocation**:

```make
# giella-core/am-shared/src-morphology-dir-include.am:134-136 (VERIFIED)
.generated/lexicon.lexc: $(GT_LEXC_ROOT) $(GT_LEXC_SRCS) $(GENERATED_LEXC_SRCS) $(GENDIR)
	echo "! automatically generated combined lexc file!!!" > $@
	cat $^ >> $@
```
```make
# giella-core/am-shared/hfst-format-include.am:23-25 (VERIFIED)
.generated/%.hfst: .generated/%.lexc $(GENDIR)
	"$(HFST_LEXC)" $(MORE_VERBOSITY) $(HFST_FORMAT) $(HFST_LEXC_FLAGS) -o $@ $<
```
The comment explaining *why* concatenation is explicit rather than relying on
`hfst-lexc`'s native multi-file support:
`giella-core/am-shared/src-morphology-dir-include.am:129-133`: *"Xerox lexc
doesn't quit on fail, and Xerox xfst can only read one lexc file (we prefer
to get an error when something doesn't work); hfst does the concatenation
automatically but by doing it explicitly we have a source file to relate
line numbers in error messages to. Foma can only read a single lexc file."*
This is purely a diagnostics/tooling-compatibility choice (foma's own
constraint is cited as one reason), not a strategy to control state-space
growth. INFERRED: for our own pg-foma emission, whole-lexicon concatenation
before a single `read lexc` is the norm GiellaLT converged on too, for the
same reason (foma reads one lexc source).

### 5.2 Phonology (rules) compiled separately, then `compose-intersect`'d against a determinized/minimized lexicon

This is where GiellaLT actually manages composition blowup, and it matches
the assignment's hypothesis precisely
(`giella-core/am-shared/src-fst-dir-include.am:263-289`, VERIFIED, full text
read):

```make
if WANT_REVERSED_INTERSECT
.generated/generator-raw-gt-desc.tmp1.hfst: morphology/.generated/lexicon.rev.hfst \
                     morphology/.generated/phonology.rev.hfst $(GENDIR)
	$(HFST_DETERMINIZE) $(HFST_FLAGS) $< \
	| $(HFST_MINIMIZE) $(HFST_FLAGS) \
	| $(HFST_COMPOSE_INTERSECT) $(COMPOSE_INTERSECT_FLAG) $(HFST_FLAGS) \
		-2 morphology/.generated/phonology.rev.hfst \
	| $(HFST_REVERSE) \
	| $(HFST_MINIMIZE) $(HFST_FLAGS) \
	-o $@
else
.generated/generator-raw-gt-desc.tmp1.hfst: morphology/.generated/lexicon.hfst \
                     morphology/.generated/phonology.compose.hfst $(GENDIR)
	$(HFST_DETERMINIZE) $(HFST_FLAGS) $< \
	| $(HFST_MINIMIZE) $(HFST_FLAGS) \
	| $(HFST_COMPOSE_INTERSECT) $(COMPOSE_INTERSECT_FLAG) $(HFST_FLAGS) \
		-2 morphology/.generated/phonology.compose.hfst \
	| $(HFST_MINIMIZE) $(HFST_FLAGS) \
	-o $@
endif
```
And the flag: `if WANT_TWOSTEP_INTERSECT / COMPOSE_INTERSECT_FLAG=--fast`
(`giella-core/am-shared/src-fst-dir-include.am:66-68`, VERIFIED) — an
explicit, opt-in "two-step" faster-but-approximate compose-intersect mode
that a language repo can turn on via its `configure.ac`/`configure.ac`-driven
Automake conditional. This is exactly the "restricted composition, not free
composition" strategy the assignment hypothesized: **determinize → minimize
→ compose-intersect (lexicon-restricted, not naive product) → minimize**, in
that fixed order, every time, for every giella-core-template language
(confirmed present verbatim in both `lang-fin` and `lang-kal`'s inherited
includes — neither overrides this file).

### 5.3 Everything downstream is a long chain of small, single-purpose filter composes

After the raw analyser is built, GiellaLT does **not** build one large
"final" transducer by composing dozens of filters into one big expression at
once. It composes them as a **linear pipeline of small, named, individually
compiled filter transducers** (`filters/remove-semantic-tags.hfst`,
`filters/remove-orthography-tags.hfst`, `orthography/inituppercase.compose.hfst`,
`orthography/spellrelax.compose.hfst`, …), each one loaded from its own
compiled file and chained with `.o.` inside a single `hfst-xfst`/`hfst-regexp2fst`
script per target
(`giella-core/am-shared/src-fst-dir-include.am:402-454`, `analyser-gt-desc.tmp.%`
target alone chains 15 separately-compiled filter transducers, VERIFIED).
INFERRED mapping to our recipe primitives: this is structurally a long
`Compose` chain of many small `Leaf`-like filter units, one per orthogonal
concern (tag removal, case handling, spelling relaxation, downcasing) —
directly expressible with our existing `Compose`/`Leaf`/`Replace` primitives,
**if** our recipe search is willing to consider chains this long (15+ compose
steps for one target transducer is normal here, not exceptional).

### 5.4 Alphabet isolation as an explicit anti-blowup tactic

`giella-core/am-shared/src-fst-dir-include.am:851-853` (VERIFIED):
```make
# also emojis should be kept separate from main morphology for efficiency
# (large alphabet slows everything down)
analyser-emojis-gt-desc.%: morphology/.generated/emojis.%
```
Direct, explicit evidence that GiellaLT engineers deliberately partition a
large-alphabet sub-vocabulary (emoji) into its own separately-compiled
transducer rather than folding it into the main morphology, specifically
because "large alphabet slows everything down." This is a concrete precedent
for a `Union`-of-separately-compiled-components strategy driven purely by
alphabet-size cost, independent of any linguistic reason to keep them
separate.

### 5.5 Optimized-lookup (`.hfstol`) conversion happens once, at the very end

```make
# giella-core/am-shared/lookup-include.am:36-38 (VERIFIED)
%.hfstol: %.hfst
	"$(HFST_FST2FST)" $(HFSTFLAGS) -w -i $< -o $@
```
Only the final, fully-composed, fully-filtered `analyser-gt-desc.hfst` /
`generator-gt-desc.hfst` targets are ever converted to `.hfstol` — every
intermediate artifact in the pipeline (lexicon, phonology, each filter) stays
in the general-purpose HFST/OpenFST-tropical format, which supports further
composition; `.hfstol` (the format `hfst-lookup`/`hfst-proc`/`hfst-optimized-lookup`
actually run against) is a terminal, non-composable format produced exactly
once per shipped transducer.

### 5.6 Mapping onto our recipe primitives

| GiellaLT mechanism | Our primitive | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Concatenate-then-`hfst-lexc` whole lexicon | `Leaf` (lexicon) | Direct |
| `determinize → minimize → compose-intersect → minimize` (lexicon ∘ rules) | `Compose` + (missing: explicit determinize/minimize steps as first-class recipe ops) | **Partial gap**: our `Compose` primitive doesn't currently expose "restricted/intersecting" composition vs. free composition as a distinct choice, nor determinize/minimize as schedulable steps between them |
| 15+-step chain of small filter composes | `Compose` chain of `Leaf`s | Direct, just needs the search to tolerate long chains |
| Alphabet-isolated sub-transducer (emoji) merged later | `Union` of independently-built components | Direct |
| Six-flag `Cmp*` compounding vocabulary | `Gate` | Direct, and suggests a **reusable, named gate template** (not a bespoke gate per grammar) is the actual GiellaLT idiom — worth considering as a library of canned `Gate` configurations rather than ad hoc per-language gates |
| Per-morpheme lexicon forking to block one recursive path (Greenlandic `_UdenTIP`) | none of our five primitives cleanly | **Gap**: this is neither `Gate` (a boolean condition) nor `Replace` — it's "duplicate this whole sub-lexicon minus one edge." Might be modelable as `Union` of a full copy and a filtered copy, but that's a workaround, not a natural fit |
| `.hfstol` conversion strictly last, once | (build-pipeline concern, not a recipe primitive) | Confirms our own plan to keep intermediate build products in a composable format and defer any lookup-optimized conversion to the final artifact only |

**Bottom line for §5**: three of five primitives (`Leaf`, `Compose`, `Union`)
map cleanly onto real GiellaLT practice; `Gate` maps cleanly onto the
compounding-flag idiom specifically, but GiellaLT's actual recursion-control
mechanism in the hardest language I examined (Greenlandic) is **lexicon
forking**, not gating — a pattern our five primitives don't naturally
express and that INFERRED may be worth a sixth primitive (something like
`Fork`/`ExceptPath`: "this sub-automaton, minus this one continuation edge")
if we hit the same recursive-affix problem at FLEx scale.

## 6. Testing and correctness at scale

### 6.1 The shared YAML + `hfst-lookup` harness

Every giella-core-template language (confirmed identical machinery in both
`lang-fin` and `lang-kal`) ships paired analysis/generation test files under
`test/gt-norm-yamls/*.yaml` and `test/gt-desc-*.yaml`, e.g.
`lang-kal/src/fst/test/gt-norm-yamls/aq-stammer_gt-norm.yaml` (VERIFIED, read
in full):

```yaml
Config:
  hfst:
    Gen: ../../../src/generator-gt-norm.hfst
    Morph: ../../../src/analyser-gt-norm.hfst
  xerox:
    Gen: ../../../src/generator-gt-norm.xfst
    Morph: ../../../src/analyser-gt-norm.xfst
    App: lookup
Tests:
  Nomener som får -aq, miniparadigme:
    ulloriaq+N+Abs+Sg: ulloriaq
    ulloriaq+N+Rel+Sg: ullorissap
    ulloriaq+N+Abl+Sg: [ullorissamit, ullorissaminngaanniit, ullorissamiit]
```
Note the `[a, b, c]` list form for a single analysis with multiple valid
surface realizations (dialectal/orthographic variants) — the test format
natively supports one-to-many expected outputs, not just one-to-one. The
runner script (`lang-kal/src/fst/test/run-gt-norm-anayaml-testcases.sh`,
VERIFIED) delegates to a **single shared script in giella-core**,
`$GIELLA_CORE/scripts/run-yaml-testcases.sh`, invoked identically by every
language repo, parameterized only by which transducer file and which
direction (`ana`/`gen`/`both`) to test — this is genuinely one shared test
harness across the whole org, not a per-language reimplementation.

### 6.2 Coverage measurement: defined precisely, but not found wired into CI for the languages I checked

`giella-core/scripts/coverage-etc.bash` (VERIFIED, full file read) is the
authoritative definition of "coverage" as GiellaLT/Divvun use the term for
the Production-tier ">90%" gate:

```bash
"$CCAT" -l "$1" "$CORPUSDIR" | "$DIVVUNRUNTIME" run -p "$ANALYSER" | "$ANSIFILTER" > "$tempfile"
tokens=$(grep -E -c '^"<' "$tempfile")
oov=$(grep -F -c ' ? ' "$tempfile")
coverage=$(echo "scale=2; (1 - $oov / $tokens) * 100" | bc | sed -e 's/[,.].*//')
```
i.e. coverage = 1 − (unanalyzed tokens / total tokens), run against a
**separate, per-language running-text corpus repo** (`corpus-$1`, e.g.
`corpus-kal`) via `divvun-runtime` and Divvun's own `corpustools`/`ccat`
extraction tool, not a shared universal test corpus. This is the closest
GiellaLT analogue to a "100% recall, no capability refusal" gate — except it
is a coverage/recall metric against real corpus text, not a synthetic
completeness guarantee, and unlike our target it explicitly tolerates <100%
(the tiers accept 80%/90% thresholds, not 100%).

However, I could **not** find a live `fst-coverage.json` badge or a
coverage-gating CI step in either `lang-fin` or `lang-kal`'s checked-out
tree (`find … -iname "*coverage*"` returned nothing in either repo, VERIFIED
2026-07-30) — only `docs/badgedata/fst-lemmacount.json`,
`fst-maturity.json`, `fst-variants.json`, `fst-version.json`,
`speller-suggestions.json`, `speller-version.json` exist as committed badge
data. INFERRED: coverage measurement is a script that exists and is
documented, and is presumably run manually or from an external
dashboard/CI system I don't have access to (not the `.github/workflows/*.yml`
present in these two repos, which are limited to `docs.yml` and
`zulip.yml` for both `lang-fin` and `lang-kal`) — i.e. **coverage is not a
merge-blocking CI gate visible in the language repo itself**, contrary to
what "regression gating in CI" might imply. This is worth treating as
"unknown/likely not enforced per-PR" rather than assuming a strict gate.

### 6.3 Plains Cree uses a third, independent test tool

As noted in §4, `lang-crk` additionally uses `fsttest`
(`https://github.com/eddieantonio/fsttest`) with TOML fixtures against
`.fomabin` artifacts — evidence that "the GiellaLT testing approach" is not
perfectly uniform across the org; the shared giella-core YAML harness is the
norm, but at least one actively-maintained polysynthetic language runs a
second, institution-specific test tool in parallel.

## 8. What this predicts for PanGloss at FLEx scale

**Tractability**: The existence proof is real and strong. Finnish alone
carries **1.15M lines of lexc source** (stems+affixes+root+compounding) and
compiles routinely through the standard giella-core pipeline into a shipped
release artifact (`grammar-fin` bundle, 61.7 MB, tagged pre-1.0 but
continuously released — most recent build tag observed
`v0.1.0-dev.20260505...`). That is roughly **an order of magnitude above**
our stated FLEx target (10⁴–10⁵ entries; Finnish's 420K-lemma badge is
4–40× that range). Greenlandic — the polysynthetic case closest to what
worries us about recursive derivation — ships as a **Production**-tier,
64K-lemma grammar with 128K lines in one affix file and 464 `LEXICON` blocks
in it alone. Neither the lexc format, `hfst-lexc`, nor foma (which both
Finnish and Greenlandic can also target, per their `Makefile.am` `CAN_FOMA`
branches) appear to hit a hard wall at this size in practice — this is
**existence evidence of tractability**, not a benchmark, since I could not
obtain actual compile-time/memory numbers (§7).

**Dominant cost, per the evidence actually found**: Not raw lexicon size —
GiellaLT's own build-rule comments identify the **lexicon ∘ rules
composition step** as the one place they've engineered specifically against
blowup (`determinize → minimize → compose-intersect → minimize`, with an
opt-in `--fast` "two-step intersect" mode, §5.2), and the one place they call
out an explicit anti-blowup tactic tied to a concrete cost mechanism
("alphabet size slows everything down" → split emoji into their own
transducer, §5.4). Neither comment concerns lexicon *size* per se; both
concern the **rule-composition and alphabet dimensions**. INFERRED for our
own plan: if we hit a scale wall at FLEx size, GiellaLT's evidence points to
"the `Compose` step against phonological rules, and alphabet/symbol-table
size" as the likely first bottlenecks — not raw lexc line count — which
argues for prioritizing our `Compose` primitive's ability to do restricted
(intersecting) composition against a determinized/minimized lexicon, over
optimizing `Leaf`/lexicon ingestion.

**What contradicts our recipe assumptions**: Two concrete gaps, both flagged
in §5.6 and worth resolving before FLEx-scale work:
1. Our `Compose` primitive doesn't currently distinguish "free composition"
   from "compose-intersect restricted to the other side's alphabet/paths" —
   GiellaLT treats these as different operations with different cost
   profiles and picks compose-intersect specifically to avoid blowup. If our
   `foma` crate backend only exposes free composition, that is a real risk
   at FLEx scale that GiellaLT's own build rules imply we would hit.
2. GiellaLT's actual mechanism for taming pathological recursive derivation
   in its hardest language (Greenlandic) is **not** expressible as `Gate`,
   `Replace`, `Union`, or `Compose` in any natural way — it is "fork this
   sub-lexicon, remove one continuation edge, and route specific derivations
   through the pruned copy" (§2, §5.6). If our own HC grammars produce
   comparably unbounded derivation chains once we scale past small reference
   grammars, we should expect to need something like this — a sixth
   primitive, or an explicit design decision that our recipe search will
   never need it because our prune-by-HC-confirmation step makes lexicon
   forking unnecessary (the FST is allowed to over-generate; HC does the
   pruning). This second point is arguably good news for our architecture
   specifically — GiellaLT has no external confirming parser and must
   therefore solve recursion control *inside* the FST; we don't, by design.
   That is the one place this research suggests our architecture may be
   structurally advantaged over GiellaLT's for exactly the axis (unbounded
   derivation) this assignment targeted.

**What we still don't know and should not assume**: compile time, memory,
and FST state/arc counts at any of these scales — GiellaLT does not publish
them and I could not derive them without actually compiling (out of scope
for this research task). Any planning number we use for "how long will a
FLEx-scale foma compile take" or "how many states will result" is currently
**unsupported by any evidence I found**, GiellaLT's or otherwise, and should
be treated as a genuine open risk, not something de-risked by this survey.
