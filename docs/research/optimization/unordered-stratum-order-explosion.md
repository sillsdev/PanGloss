# unordered-stratum-order-explosion

Ledger entry (format: `.claude/skills/optimization-research/references/ledger-format.md`). Back to the [ledger](README.md).

- **Status:** measured (2026-11-27). No issue yet; a `sillsdev/machine` issue with the tables below is the next step. 2026-11-27: oracle study and state-repeat census done (below); the lever is re-aimed at synthesis of the expanded variants.
- **Blowup it targets:** a stratum whose morphological rules are `morphologicalRuleOrder="unordered"` (the FieldWorks default) explores every combination and order of its rules. One heavy Bantu grammar has an unordered stratum of 22 morphological rules. Subsets of non-interacting prefix rules (noun-class-like markers, an applicative, a causative) are re-derived in every order, and each combination word re-runs the whole template battery. Work grows with the number of orderings, not with the number of distinct rule sets.
- **Measured (release 0.35.0, which has the shape graph; one thread, work steps):** the same grammar with only that stratum's attribute switched to `linear`:
  - 6 costliest parsing words: 11.4x, 8.3x, 7.1x, 4.9x, 3.8x and 3.1x less work (the top word 88.6M to 7.8M); signatures identical on all 6.
  - 40 random parsing words: 48.5M to 22.6M (2.15x overall, median 1.7x, max 16.7x).
  - 872 words (803 with a signature, 69 without; work 50k to 8M each): total 673.5M to 298.8M (2.25x).
  - One more heavy word still hits its cap under `linear`, so ordering is not the whole story.
  - Command shape: `pangloss batch <grammar.xml> <words> out.tsv --threads=1 --work-columns --work-cap 400000000 --word-mem-cap-mib 100`, once on the grammar and once with `morphologicalRuleOrder="unordered"` replaced by `"linear"` in that stratum.
- **Oracle: `linear` is NOT equivalent.** 5 of the 872 words differ: 3 lose their only parse and 2 lose duplicate readings. Any fix must reproduce those 5 words exactly as the unordered run does; they are the acceptance test for commutation reasoning, and "treat the stratum as linear" is rejected by them.
- **Oracle study (2026-11-27, measured):** the 5 differing words are not about commuting rules. Three lose their only reading under `linear` because the reading unapplies a free rule before a template, an interleaving that linear analysis never visits; two keep the same distinct readings but lose order duplicates (10 to 6 and 8 to 6). Any change must keep both.
- **Where the work goes (measured, three heavy words):** the analysis-only ceiling, if every order variant vanished, is 1.29x to 1.71x work because synthesis is 53% to 74% of work. The funnel on the heaviest word: 592,128 analysis nodes, 31,733 canonicals sent to lookup, 16,462 root hits over only 68 distinct shapes, 147,053 alternatives held, 191,716 variants synthesised, 2 readings. 99.9% of synthesis work is on variants that die.
- **Census (measured, see [state-tabled-continuations](state-tabled-continuations.md)):** 0.59 of stratum work is under repeat arrivals of an equal state key on this grammar, but the allomorph-on-trail class is 79% of it and rule order only 14%.
- **Mechanism (candidates, none built; options 1 and 2 below are weakened by the study, see the next step):**
  1. Commutation proof (rejected 2026-11-27: of 22 free rules 19 are prefixes and 2 are suffixes, same-edge pairs never commute, at most 38 of 231 pairs are candidates, estimated 1.0x to 1.1x): rules with disjoint input/output footprint (feature reads and writes, MPR, stem names, rule-count gates, template state) are explored in one canonical order and the other orders are emitted by permuting the trail, never dropped. This is the same family as [static-commutation-sleep-sets](README.md#static-commutation-sleep-sets).
  2. Reuse one template-battery result per (shape, applied-rule set): this is [state-tabled-continuations](state-tabled-continuations.md) at the template level.
  3. (Done) the census says 0.59 of the stratum work on this grammar sits under repeat arrivals; the interpretation is pending.
- **Where in PanGloss:** `rust/crates/pg-rules/src/stratum.rs` (`run_mrule_cascade`, `apply_mrules`, `apply_templates`), `rust/crates/pg-rules/src/cascade.rs` (`combination_prepared`, `combination_rec`); the grammar attribute is read in `rust/crates/pg-grammar/src/load.rs` (`morphologicalRuleOrder`). In C#: `CombinationRuleCascade.ApplyRules`, `AnalysisStratumRule.Apply` in sillsdev/machine. Same algorithm in C#, so this is a Machine issue first.
- **Expected gain:** measured upper bound 2.25x total on this grammar (3x to 11x on its heaviest words), because `linear` removes the ordering work and also some real parses; a sound fix recovers part of it. Largest single factor on the slowest corpus (about 5G steps, 9 timeouts and 28 memory-capped words in the v0.35.0 sweep).
- **Parity risk:** medium to high. Trail order is part of `WordKey`, so reordered arrivals are distinct output words; the set, first-seen order, duplicate counts and `dedup_alternative` pruning must be identical. Gate: `parse_compare.py --multiset` on the five heavy grammars plus the conformance suite, plus the 5 differing words above.
- **Memo/cache?** Option 1: no (a precomputed relation). Option 2: yes (a per-word table of expanded continuations; needs owner approval, see the policy section).
- **Prior art:** partial-order reduction with sleep sets (Godefroid 1996, cited from memory); dynamic partial-order reduction (Flanagan and Godefroid, POPL 2005); tabling (see state-tabled-continuations).
- **Issues:** none yet. Related: machine#653, #635, PanGloss #66.
- **Next step:** research synthesis of the expanded variants (shared rule prefixes, early death): see [synthesis-of-dying-variants](README.md#synthesis-of-dying-variants). A replay table keyed on the analysis state is analysis-only (ceiling above) and needs owner approval. Note that the cascade keys on `WordKey`, not `AnalysisStateKey`, so a replay table would need its own state key or interned id per node.
