● Two different things, and only one of them is benign.

  1. HC_ENUM_ENTRY_BUDGET — pure space. 200,730 composite lexc entries against a
  200,000 default cap. EnumerationBudget
  (rust/crates/pg-foma/src/morphotactics.rs:139) is a fail-fast guard against
  writing a multi-GB .lexc and OOMing foma's apply_up. Nothing semantic; raising
  it changes no analysis. Mbugwe is 0.4% over — it just lands on the wrong side
  of a line calibrated on Aweti.

  2. --allow-unproven — a real feature-combination gap, and it UNDER-generates.

  CircumfixStructuralCompositePredicate
  (rust/crates/pg-foma/src/capability.rs:2364) returns Refuse when any observed
  CircumfixOutputAction has structural_composite_attempted == false. Mbugwe has
  one: mrule 166 allomorph #0, a LHS-material-dropping output action that
  emit::is_structural_rule (emit.rs:1791) does not route through
  build_structural_composites.

  The consequence is under-generation, not over-generation. The predicate's own
  doc says it: "the real compiler already honestly skips this exact allomorph, 
  never a silent wrong compile, but a grammar depending on it must be refused 
  rather than silently missing recall." The allomorph is reported uncovered and
  never enters the net; the proposer therefore never emits a candidate using it,
  and since the pipeline is FST-propose + HC-confirm with no full-HC fallback,
  a candidate that is never proposed can never be confirmed. Those analyses are
  silently gone. Over-generation would be the harmless direction — confirm
  prunes it. This is the other one.

  So: Refuse is doing exactly its job, and --allow-unproven converted "I won't
  answer" into "I'll answer, possibly with holes." That's why the run banner
  says the output is recall-unsafe.

  3. Separately — a gap that hits both engines. Those two import warnings aren't
  cosmetic. build_affix_rule (rust/crates/pg-grammar/src/compile/affixes.rs:61)
  drops any MorphType::Circumfix entry outright: "circumfix cross-product 
  allomorphs not implemented; entry skipped." Two Mbugwe lexical entries never
  make it into the grammar at all, so the HC oracle is missing them too — which
  means HC is not a clean recall baseline for this project either.

  For your coverage work: Mbugwe is a real-grammar witness for the Refuse branch
  of CharacteristicKind::CircumfixOutputAction, plus circumfix cross-product
  allomorphs at the pg-grammar layer. Both are circumfix-shaped, which is
  unsurprising for Bantu.

  I can put a number on the actual recall loss for free — both passes write
  signatures, so a word-by-word FST-vs-HC diff over the corpus measures exactly
  how many analyses the FST path drops. HC is at 117/1638 (43.6% hitting the 20s
  cap), so that comparison lands when it finishes.

● Yes — this helps a lot, and it's worth being precise about why: your heuristic
  is exactly the formal criterion the architecture has been circling without
  naming.

  Why it's right, in FST terms. Long-distance agreement is the classic thing
  finite-state morphology is bad at: to enforce "prefix i pairs with suffix i"
  across the stem, the automaton must remember the prefix choice through every
  stem arc, which multiplies state count by the number of classes remembered —
  and nested circumfixes multiply it per level (k pairings, depth 5 → k⁵). This
  is so canonical that flag diacritics were invented in lexc/xfst specifically
  for circumfix agreement. But we have something strictly stronger than flag
  diacritics: HC confirm already re-derives the full analysis. So "check
  intra-side ordering and local transformations in the FST; leave cross-stem
  type/number/phonology matching to HC" is not a compromise — it's putting each
  check in the engine that does it natively. And it's recall-safe by 
  construction: dropping a constraint from the proposer only ever adds
  candidates (superset), and over-generation is the harmless direction — confirm
  prunes it. That's precisely the existing ConfirmOnly disposition, now with a
  principled story for which constraints get dropped.

  Can we match them easily? Split the question the way your heuristic does:

  - A single circumfix: pairing is free. The cross-product lowering we just
  built puts both halves inside one rule application ([Insert(pfx+), Copy(stem),
  Insert(+sfx)]) — the FST never has to "remember" anything across the stem,
  because the pairing is inside one arc-path insertion. No blowup; nothing to
  relax. Mbugwe's two entries are 1×1, so unit 1 as implemented is the right
  shape.
  - Nested/composed circumfixes and big cross-products: this is where the eager
  composite enumerator pays k^depth — and it's exactly the shape of the
  200,730-entry explosion (Bantu subject×TAM×… products). Under your heuristic
  the proposer emits prefix-side material and suffix-side material independently
  — each side a plain slot chain enforcing its own ordering and its own
  adjacent transformations — and the composite space factorizes from a product
  (k^depth) to a sum (k·depth). Mismatched pairs get proposed and then die at
  confirm. The templated (P6) backend is already built this way: PrefixSlots and
  SuffixSlots are separate continuation chains, and nothing in a lexc chain
  ties a specific prefix-slot filler to a specific suffix-slot filler except
  morphosyntactic features — which is HC's job anyway.

  Is it a good backend switch? Yes — I'd make it the routing predicate. The perf
  change currently says "route cascade-family grammars to the templated backend
  if measurement supports it." Your heuristic sharpens that into something
  testable per-grammar: route to the slot-chain backend exactly when every 
  long-distance dependency in the grammar is agreement-only (confirm-checkable) 
  rather than material-determining. The one caveat that keeps this honest: if a
  suffix's form depends on which prefix was chosen (long-distance material
  determination, not agreement), the factorized proposer must over-propose both
  variants rather than silently pick one — still recall-safe, but it's the case
  the containment gate must specifically witness. And the cost that must be
  measured, not assumed: relaxing pairing raises candidate volume at confirm,
  and confirm time is where mbugwe's worst words already hurt (45s mmbere) — the
  routing measurement already records candidate volume, so the tradeoff lands
  in the decision record.
